# SOP-001: Regulation E — Unauthorized Electronic Fund Transfers (Debit & ACH)

**Document ID:** `sop-reg-e-unauthorized-eft`  
**Version:** `2026.1`  
**Category:** `regulation_e`  
**Effective Date:** 2026-01-01  
**Owner:** Card Disputes & Regulatory Compliance

## 1. Scope & Covered Transactions

This Standard Operating Procedure governs consumer claims of unauthorized Electronic Fund Transfers (EFTs) under Regulation E (12 CFR Part 1005), including consumer debit card purchases, ATM withdrawals, P2P wallet transfers, and unauthorized ACH debits. Business/commercial accounts are excluded and follow the Commercial Treasury Dispute SOP.

An **unauthorized EFT** is a transfer from a consumer's account initiated by a person other than the consumer without actual authority to initiate the transfer and from which the consumer receives no benefit.

## 2. Consumer Liability Tiers & Reporting Windows

Consumer liability depends on whether an access device (physical debit card) was lost/stolen and how quickly the cardholder notifies the institution after learning of the loss or receiving their periodic statement.

| Scenario | Notification Window | Maximum Consumer Liability | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lost or Stolen Debit Card** | Within **2 business days** of learning of loss/theft | **$50** (or amount of unauthorized transfers before notice, whichever is less) | If cardholder has zero-liability card network protection, apply $0 unless gross negligence is documented. |
| **Lost or Stolen Debit Card** | More than **2 business days** after learning of loss, up to **60 calendar days** after statement transmittal | **$500** (up to $50 for first 2 business days + remaining unauthorized transfers up to $500 total) | Must document when consumer actually learned of the loss/theft. |
| **Failure to Report Statement Error** | More than **60 calendar days** after periodic statement transmittal | **Unlimited liability** for transfers occurring *after* day 60 that could have been prevented | Applies only to unauthorized transfers after the 60-day window closes. |
| **Card Not Lost or Stolen (Card-Absent / Skimming / Account Takeover)** | Within **60 calendar days** of statement transmittal | **$0** | Consumers have $0 liability if the physical access device was not lost or stolen and error is reported within 60 days. |

## 3. Investigation & Provisional Credit SLA Timelines

Dispute analysts must strictly adhere to Regulation E statutory deadlines:

1. **Initial Investigation Window:** Complete the investigation within **10 business days** of receiving the consumer's error notice (oral or written).
2. **Provisional Credit Requirement:** If the investigation cannot be completed within **10 business days**, the institution may extend the investigation to **45 calendar days**, provided it **credits the consumer's account provisionally** within 10 business days for the full disputed amount (less up to $50 if applicable).
3. **New Accounts, POS, and Foreign Transactions (90-Day Extension):** For transfers involving a **point-of-sale (POS) debit card transaction**, a **foreign-initiated transfer**, or an account opened within the past **30 calendar days**, the extended investigation window is **90 calendar days** (instead of 45 days). For new accounts (<= 30 days), the institution has **20 business days** (instead of 10) to issue provisional credit.
4. **Error Determination & Final Notice:**
   - If an error **did** occur: correct the error within **1 business day** after determining an error occurred and make provisional credit final.
   - If **no** error occurred: send a written explanation within **3 business days** of completing the investigation, provide copies of relied-upon documents upon request, and give **5 business days** advance notice before debiting back any provisional credit (honoring any overdrafts/checks during that 5-day notice window without NSF fees).
