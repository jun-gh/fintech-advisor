# SOP-002: Regulation Z — Credit Card Billing Errors & Unauthorized Use

**Document ID:** `sop-reg-z-credit-card-disputes`  
**Version:** `2026.1`  
**Category:** `regulation_z`  
**Effective Date:** 2026-01-01  
**Owner:** Credit Card Operations & Compliance

## 1. Scope & Liability Cap for Unauthorized Credit Card Use

Under Regulation Z (12 CFR § 1026.12 & § 1026.13), consumer liability for **unauthorized use** of a credit card is strictly capped at **$50** (and **$0** if the cardholder notifies the issuer before unauthorized use occurs, or if the transaction is card-not-present). Under card network Zero Liability policies, eligible consumers are credited $0 liability unless fraud/collusion by the cardholder is proven.

## 2. Billing Error Notice & Statutory Deadlines

A **billing error** includes unauthorized charges, charges for undelivered or misrepresented goods/services, math errors, and failure to post payments or credits.

1. **Cardholder Reporting Window:** The cardholder must submit a billing error notice within **60 calendar days** after the institution transmitted the first periodic statement reflecting the error.
2. **Written Acknowledgment SLA:** Send a written acknowledgment of the billing error notice within **30 calendar days** of receipt (unless the error is fully resolved within those 30 days).
3. **Final Resolution SLA:** Resolve the dispute within **two complete billing cycles**, and **no later than 90 calendar days** after receiving the billing error notice.

## 3. Restrictions During an Open Billing Error Investigation

While a Regulation Z billing error investigation is pending, Operations and Collections **must NOT**:
- Attempt to collect the disputed amount or any related finance/late charges.
- Report the disputed amount as delinquent to any credit reporting agency (CRA).
- Restrict or close the cardholder's account solely because the cardholder exercised billing error rights in good faith.
- Automatically deduct the disputed amount from a linked deposit account without explicit post-notice authorization.

## 4. Claims & Defenses Against a Merchant (12 CFR § 1026.12(c))

A cardholder may assert non-fraud claims or defenses (e.g., defective merchandise) against the issuer for credit card purchases if **all three conditions** are met:
1. The cardholder made a **good-faith attempt** to resolve the issue with the merchant first.
2. The transaction amount exceeded **$50**.
3. The transaction occurred in the cardholder's **home state or within 100 miles** of their current designated mailing address (note: for online/e-commerce transactions, internal policy applies the cardholder's billing address as the point of sale unless superseded by network chargeback rights).
